Corporate Profile: 
Talatu Plc 
NGX: TALATU  | (₦1,500 = US$1)
Corporate Overview
Talatu Plc is a Nigerian media and entertainment group founded in 1962 as Jaridah Media Company, it has grown over six decades from a single northern daily newspaper into a business spanning television, publishing, film and series production, streaming, sports broadcasting, gaming, and consumer licensing, with operations in eight markets plus an Egyptian associate.
Governance and ownership
The Alhassan family controls 59.6% of Talatu, Anwar Alhassan holds 31.0% through Westbridge Asset Management Limited and Westbridge Investments Limited, His sisters Amira and Aisha hold 28.6% through the Alhassan Family Office. Under the 2018 shareholders’ agreement, none of the three vehicles votes on the appointment of the CEO, which is reserved to independent directors.
The Board of Directors comprises 11 members, chaired by Anwar, with governance supported by five standing board committees: Audit, Risk, Governance & Nomination, Remuneration, and Editorial. Day-to-day corporate operations are led by Chief Executive Officer.

Corporate History, 1930–2026
              Roots and founding, 1930–1979
1930s: The Alhassan family runs a trading business in groundnuts, hides, and textiles across Zaria. 
On 7 May 1935, Faisal is born to parents Mahmoud and Talatu, He grows up in the family business and attends school in Zaria until 1948.
1948–54: Faisal attends Barewa College, where he edits the school publication and gains his first exposure to the power of the press and public discourse.
1954-58: On a Northern Region scholarship, Faisal studies Political Science at the London School of Economics, writing for the student paper and returns home in 1958. 
1959: Elected to the federal House of Representatives in the December elections on the Northern People’s Congress (NPC) ticket.
A traditionalist and protégé of Sir Ahmadu Bello, Faisal becomes a prominent advocate for the Northern establishment defending regional autonomy, native authority institutions, and the Sardauna’s modernization program against southern political pressure.
1962: Using family money, Faisal sets up Jaridah Media Company in Kaduna with two printing machines. He takes the role of Chairman & Publisher while appointing journalist Mukhtar Bello as Editor-in-Chief. 
With Faisal still serving in parliament, The daily paper’s editorial line favors peace, regional interests and gradual reform.
1966: The January coup ends the civilian government and kills several of his political allies. 
Faisal loses his seat, leaves elective politics and runs Jaridah Media Company full-time from Kaduna with Mukhtar Bello.
1967–70: During the Civil War, Jaridah backs the Federal Government and daily circulation reaches 90,000. The paper clashes with military censors and two editors are detained. Faisal moves company headquarters to Lagos in 1969 to be closer to the national market and the federal government. 
1973–75: Jaridah launches a Sunday edition and a Hausa-language weekly. Government print advertising supports the business, and the paper is seen as aligned with the ruling establishment, a view that persists for decades.
1979: Nigeria returns to civilian rule under the Second Republic, Jaridah supports conservative candidates.
Printing 140,000 copies a day, The Jaridah is the third-largest daily in the country.
Expansion and defiance, 1980–1999
1984: A military press decree leads to the imprisonment of a Jaridah reporter for 14 months. Jaridah Media pays for his legal defense. Mukhtar Bello runs a blank column bearing the reporter’s name on the front page every day until his release.
1984–90: Faisal acquires the Lagos daily The Comet (1984) and The Continental (1987), promising both full editorial independence. Both take editorial lines that conflict with The Jaridah’s conservative position.
1986: A currency devaluation and a newsprint shortage leave the company in debt. Faisal mortgages his family properties in Zaria and lays off 300 employees.
1992–93: Jaridah Media wins a national broadcast license and establishes its broadcasting division, Comet Broadcasting Corporation (CBC) led by Yero Gella. Anwar joins as a planning analyst on the broadcast development team. The group launches television station CBC 1 in 1993. 
June 12, 1993: The annulment of the presidential election splits the Group’s titles. The Jaridah’s editorial counsels restraint and a negotiated return, which readers take as support for the interim arrangement. 
The Comet runs a front page demanding that the apparent winner, M.K.O. Abiola, be declared. Faisal, bound by his promise of editorial independence intervenes in neither.
 In November, Abacha’s takeover hardens both positions. CBC 1, on air for a few months, covers the crisis in its news bulletins.
1994–95: The broadcast arm concentrates on news and market coverage. CBC Evening News debuts on CBC 1 in 1994 under Head of News Sani Bala and tops national ratings within a year. The daily financial programme CBC Business follows in 1995.
1996: The Comet runs an investigation into an Abacha-era oil revenue policy. When the regime demands a retraction, Faisal and Mukhtar both refuse. State security agents seal the printing presses and suspend CBC’s broadcast license. 
Facing arrest, Faisal appoints administrator Dauda Makarfi as Jaridah Media CEO and leaves for London. Mukhtar remains in Lagos under military surveillance and runs the newsroom. 
The regime’s retraction demand names CBC’s executives, and founding CEO Yero Gella resigns after the suspension. Sani Bala is appointed CEO of CBC.
1997-98: Faisal founds Westbridge Asset Management in London as the family’s holding company. It funds staff on half-salaries until the ban is lifted. 
In 1998, after Abacha’s death, Faisal returns from exile and CBC’s licence is restored.
Diversification and listing, 2000–2009
2001: CBC Business is spun off from CBC 1 as a standalone 24-hour channel. Anwar is named its Head, with Aliyu Mania as General Manager Commercial and Operations.
 It becomes the country’s leading market-news network. The company also launches Capital Register as its financial print weekly.
2003–05: The company acquires Diplomatic Dispatch (2003), launches CBC 2 (2004), National Review of Affairs and CBC Kids (2005), and enters book publishing and film distribution. 
State governors file defamation suits and regulators bring actions against CBC. All are settled or reduced on appeal, and legal costs rise.
2005: The Federal Government confers the national honour of Commander of the Order of the Federal Republic (CFR) on both Faisal Alhassan and Mukhtar Bello, in recognition of their services to Nigerian journalism and the press.
2006: The company lists on the Nigerian Stock Exchange and is renamed Talatu Plc, raising ₦26bn (roughly US$200m). 
Faisal retains a 68% stake through Westbridge Asset Management, now redomiciled in Nigeria. Ebenezer Ogunleye succeeds Dauda as CEO. 
The business splits into specialized arms: CBC, led by CEO Sani Bala, Jaridah Publishing under Managing Director & Publisher Patrick Ibe, and Odyssey Entertainment led by CEO Tope Majekodunmi.
Ironwood Pictures is founded as Odyssey’s theatrical label, built from the film distribution unit that started in 2003-05. Film executive Ovie Ejiro is its founding CEO, reporting to Odyssey’s Tope Majekodunmi.
The Editorial  Charter is adopted at listing, drafted by Mukhtar Bello with Faisal’s backing, with an Editorial Committee reporting to the board. It guarantees editors freedom from owner interference but permits titles to keep a house editorial line.
2007:  Faisal vetoes proposals to expand Odyssey into Northern Nigeria's growing Kannywood film industry. Facing warnings from conservative clerics and regional cultural groups over commercial cinema backed by an establishment Northern family, Faisal refuses to court controversy over religion and censorship.
Now Senior Vice President of News and Business, Anwar oversees investigations that draw objections from ruling politicians and advertisers. Under pressure from broadcast head Sani Bala, Ogunleye moves Anwar out of hard news, transferring him to Odyssey Entertainment as Chief Content Officer with broad creative freedom to build out television and film.
2008: Capital Register and The Continental break a joint investigation alleging systemic embezzlement and rigged player selections within the national basketball federation. 
Weeks of sustained reporting leads the federation president and several board members to resign. The investigation is soon overshadowed by the global financial crisis, which reduces group advertising revenue by 18% and Talatu’s share price by 45%.
Anwar, as Odyssey’s Chief Content Officer, founds Talatu Studios as an in-house series label and hires Bode Fasanya  as its founding head. Its first series, the courtroom drama Harmattan Court, premieres on CBC 2 in 2009. Zuri and the Talking Drum follows in 2010.
2009: Approaching his mid-seventies, Faisal formally steps down from active board duties to become Chairman Emeritus. Longtime confidant and former CEO Dauda Makarfi is appointed Chairman of Talatu after the crash. 
While Sani Bala retires after more than a decade as CEO of CBC to become Chairman of the CBC Board; Aliyu Mania, head of CBC Business, succeeds him as CEO of CBC. 
Mukhtar Bello, the board’s senior non-executive director, is named Vice-Chairman, continuing as the company’s senior editorial figure.
Jaridah Publishing buys  Port Harcourt daily People’s Chronicle for about ₦2.1bn (roughly US$14m), acquired at a post-crash price. It gives the Group a Niger Delta newsroom.
As Faisal becomes Chairman Emeritus, he funds the Talatu Foundation, with his wife Fauziya as founding chair.
Streaming, Africa and sport, 2010–2020
2010: Talatu Licensing launches to manage the Group’s IP and consumer products, with its first major property the children’s animated series Zuri and the Talking Drum produced by Talatu Studios and broadcast on CBC Kids. 
Concurrently, Anwar leads Odyssey’s production of  The Long Ballot, an investigative documentary detailing systemic vote-buying in an off-cycle governorship race. Two senators demand broadcast sanctions,  Ogunleye who had moved Anwar out of news in 2007 rather than lose him, publicly defends the film.
2011: Talatu establishes the Employee Share Trust, issuing new shares equal to about 2% of capital to align staff with the post-crash recovery. The family’s stake dilutes to about 66.7%.
CBC World launches as the international news and global affairs channel, aimed at Africa and the diaspora. Damilola Shonibare, then a CBC News executive, is its founding head. The channel also lays the groundwork for Talatu Africa Media in 2012.
Ironwood’s first theatrical hit, The Surveyor’s Daughter (2011), is a land-dispute drama that grosses five times its budget and establishes the label.
2012: The Group forms Talatu Africa Media to anchor regional operations, appointing Itunu Amaegbe, its SVP of Regional Operations, as founding CEO.
Anwar is promoted to CEO of Odyssey Entertainment as founding chief Tope Majekodunmi departs to launch an independent production house David Saro, formerly a vice president for international content at Sky, joins Anwar as Odyssey’s Chief Content Officer, hired ahead of the streaming launch.
2013: Talatu Africa Media acquires South Africa’s Baseline Motion Pictures for US$60m to scale Odyssey’s production capacity, Baseline Motion Pictures acquisition is partly funded by placing new shares (about 3%) to institutions, which keeps debt down after the 2008 crash. 
CBC launches Pinnacle Sports as its sports channel.
Faisal favours equity financing over additional borrowing and the family stake falls to about 64.7%. This also brings Marina Capital, First Alliance and Highwater onto the register.
2014: Odyssey launches streaming platform Odyssey+. 
In print, The Comet teams up with People's Chronicle to uncover an unreported Niger Delta spill by multinational oil company Rivermark Energy using satellite imagery, triggering US$240m in regulatory penalties. 
Rivermark blacklists the group: it withdraws high-margin corporate sponsorships from CBC Business and discontinues statutory public filings in Capital Register, costing Talatu an estimated ₦4bn across two years.
2015: Talatu Africa Media acquires Signal Television (STV) for US$75m, after a multi-country regulatory review that requires local board representation and content quotas. Local currency devaluations and slow pay-TV adoption keep the asset unprofitable for the next four years.
2016: CBC buys sports-rights agency and syndicator Fieldhouse Rights, folding it into Pinnacle Sports to build an in-house rights acquisition and distribution arm.
Baseline’s founder completes his earn-out and leaves. The studio moves from tentpole features to production services and drama for Odyssey+.
Meanwhile, Odyssey+ records a ₦9bn write-down from costly foreign licensed content, the library strategy Anwar championed at the 2014 launch over internal warnings that local originals would be cheaper to sustain. 
He takes responsibility before the board, then re-engineers the streaming strategy, pivoting toward low-cost local originals and telco data bundles to reverse the cash burn and engineer a subscriber recovery 
With the naira float inflating newsprint costs and print advertising in decline, Jaridah Publishing launches a metered digital subscription, led by Capital Register’s paying business readers.
With Faisal in declining health, Westbridge sells about 5.1% of Talatu in a block placement to Marina Capital, First Alliance and Highwater bringing the family’s combined stake to its present 59.6% and lifting the three funds' combined holdings to 15.7%. The proceeds add to Westbridge’s existing investment portfolio (property, banking, technology) outside Talatu stock.
Fauziya Alhassan, founding chair of the Talatu Foundation, passes away. Aisha succeeds her as chair.
Mukhtar Bello retires from the board after fifty-four years, the last seven as Vice-Chairman. The board grants him the title Vice-Chairman Emeritus, a ceremonial office he holds until his death. 
2017: Anwar is elevated to President, Studios and Entertainment, overseeing content strategy across the group. David Saro succeeds him as Odyssey CEO.
Yusuf Abubakar, then Editor-in-Chief of The Jaridah, is appointed President, News and Publishing, covering Jaridah Publishing and the group’s news output. Ibrahim Gwarzo succeeds him as Editor-in-Chief of The Jaridah.
Pinnacle Sports wins exclusive Nigerian rights to Super Eagles home internationals and a top European league package through 2024.	
A Senate committee chaired by Ebikabowei Tamuno of Bayelsa opens hearings on the Group’s dominance of national team and European league rights and its integrated model across broadcast, streaming and print. Ebenezer Ogunleye and CBC CEO Aliyu Mania defend the rights deal and the integrated model, with Anwar speaking to content integration. The NBC clears Pinnacle’s rights with conditions, requiring part of the package to be sublicensed to rival broadcasters.
2018:  Faisal Alhassan dies after a prolonged illness. His 59.6% passes to his three children. Anwar inherits 31.0%, held through Westbridge Asset Management and the newly incorporated Westbridge Investments Limited. Amira and Aisha inherit 28.6% between them, held in the new Alhassan Family Office, with Amira joining the Talatu board as its representative. 
The wider non-Talatu portfolio moves into Northstar Investments Limited, held jointly by all three. The siblings then sign the shareholders’ agreement (see Governance).
2019: Ebenezer Ogunleye announces his retirement,  Pursuant to the 2018 shareholders' agreement, Anwar’s two vehicles and the Alhassan Family Office do not take part in the appointment of a CEO.
The independent directors, convened by Dauda Makarfi,  runs a formal selection process and appoints Yusuf Abubakar, then President, News and Publishing, as CEO.
The President, News and Publishing role is eliminated, with divisional CEOs now reporting directly to the CEO. David Saro is elevated from Odyssey CEO to Chief Operating Officer of Talatu  and Bode Fasanya, head of Talatu Studios, succeeds him at Odyssey, an internal successor takes over Talatu Studios. 
Abubakar names Etim Effiong, SVP Corporate Development and M&A as CFO.
Abubakar's pitch to the board is diversification away from politically risky journalism, with a theme park and resort business built on the Group's children's IP as its centerpiece.
Thabo Mokoena, formerly chief operating officer of a Johannesburg-based pay-TV group, succeeds Itunu Amaegbe as CEO of Talatu Africa Media.
2020 COVID-19 lockdowns cut ad revenue by 22% and freeze live sport, while Odyssey+ grows subscriptions 60%, The family drama The Compound becomes the platform’s most-streamed original during lockdown.
The board incorporates Talatu Parks & Resorts Limited and approves Zuri World, a roughly US$150m park and resort on a Lekki-Epe site, funded by about US$70m of Group cash and US$80m of dollar-denominated bank debt. Under the approval, the CEO may sign purchase contracts of up to US$4m each without returning to the board. 
The Risk Committee proposes the signing authority, and the board carries it. Abubakar and Etim Effiong vote in favour of both the project and the authority, along with most of the independent directors. David Saro, as COO, supports the project but votes against the delegated signing authority, arguing that contracts should return to the board above a cumulative limit; his objection is minuted. Dr. Bukola Adewale is the only independent director to vote against the authority. Anwar Alhassan votes against both the project and the accompanying cuts to investigative and documentary budgets, and his dissent is minuted. The Alhassan Family Office, represented by Amira Alhassan, abstains.
Abubakar cuts investigative documentary and content budgets to fund the project, Anwar opposes them in the boardroom. Lockdowns halt construction, and naira weakness inflates the cost of imported rides and equipment. Between August and December, eleven contracts for imported rides and equipment are signed.
Signal Television (STV) posts its first annual profit, five years after its acquisition, STV renegotiates the content quotas set in the 2015 approvals, sells advertising across all six markets as a single regional buy, and moves distribution onto telco bundles. Lockdown viewing and lower operating costs help the result.
CBC News’s coverage of the #EndSARS protests and the Lekki shootings, which relies heavily on unverified citizen footage, draws an NBC sanction and a conditions letter. The Editorial Committee’s inquiry traces part of the failure to the verification desk, which had been thinned by the year’s cuts to investigative and documentary budgets. 
The Anwar era, 2021–2026
2021: In Q1 the external auditors’ management letter lists the eleven contracts, each just under US$4m and together about US$40m in dollar commitments. None appear in the capex reports, which track cash paid only. With the naira weakness, the park’s cost is heading toward US$190m. The board suspends the project with the park about 40% built. The Group takes a write-down of about US$80m, covering built cost and contract cancellation penalties, and its share price falls by about 25%. 
The institutional holders who backed Abubakar in 2019 (Marina Capital, First Alliance, Highwater) withdraw their support. The Westbridge vehicles and the Alhassan Family Office vote to remove Abubakar as a director, and the institutions do not oppose.
Dauda Makarfi steps down as Chairman before a shareholder vote on Abubakar’s removal. Yusuf Abubakar is removed as CEO for cause, the board finding that he repeatedly signed contracts just under the US$4m signing limit and failed to report the cumulative commitments to the board, so that about US$40m in dollar liabilities went unreported while the park’s cost rose. He is then removed as a director by shareholder resolution. He disputes both and sues at the National Industrial Court, claiming his contractual severance. 
A committee of independent directors, presided over by Dr. Bukola Adewale, appoints David Saro as CEO. The family votes instead in favour of Anwar’s election as Executive Chairman.
By June AGM, Talatu’s CFO Etim Effiong and several board members  resign from the company. Femi Ayodeji, from the Group treasury team, becomes CFO and joins the board with new board members appointed.
Saro names Aliyu Mania, then CEO of CBC, as President & COO, Ovie Ejiro, then CEO of Ironwood Pictures, Chief Content Officer, and the board ratifies both appointments in new corporate restructuring. 
Damilola Shonibare, head of CBC World, becomes acting CEO of CBC, Anwar's Studios and Entertainment presidency ends upon his appointment as Executive Chairman and is abolished.
The promotions open vacancies across the Group, filled by internal appointment: new heads at Ironwood Pictures, CBC World and Pinnacle Sports, and a new head of Odyssey+.
Zuri World’s debt is restructured, Talatu Parks & Resorts is wound up, the Zuri IP returns to Talatu Licensing, and the cleared land becomes the Southstone Campus. 
Saro moves to consolidate the Group's scattered operations, acquiring adjoining parcels to expand the site to 85 hectares on the Lekki–Epe Expressway. He breaks ground on the campus as a purpose-built operational headquarters and production hub.
2022: Sani Bala retires as Chairman of CBC,  Patrick Ibe retires from Jaridah Publishing and Justice Rabi Garba  is appointed CBC chair while Ibrahim Gwarzo, Editor-in-Chief of The Jaridah since 2017, is appointed Jaridah chairman. Both divisional boards are refreshed under the amended Charter, Damilola Shonibare is confirmed as CEO of CBC, and Folarin Adegoke becomes Managing Director & Publisher of Jaridah.
The Editorial Committee concludes a two-year review of the 2006 Charter, prompted by the theme park episode and by wider questions about how commercial and management considerations had affected editorial decisions. It finds that the Charter protected editors from direct instruction but not from budgetary and commercial pressure, a point illustrated by the 2020 cuts to investigative programming.
The review produces the Neutrality and Separation Amendment, drafted under Angela Nwana and sponsored through the board by David Saro. The Amendment ends party endorsements across all Group titles and platforms, a practice Jaridah had followed since 1962. It separates advertising from editorial decision-making, sets a minimum budget for investigative work and for CBC’s verification desk, and makes the Editorial Committee a full board committee. Angela Nwana joins the board, and Hadiza Yerima joins as Remuneration chair.
The Jaridah’s print sales fall 11%, and Northern state governments withdraw about ₦3bn in advertising. A regulator’s compliance inquiry into CBC is cleared the following year.
Abubakar’s suit, which argues that every contract sat inside his signing authority and that the board never required cumulative reporting, settles for ₦2.4bn, with no admission of liability and a non-disparagement clause. 
2023 Vice-Chairman Emeritus Mukhtar Bello passes away in Kaduna at the age of 88.
Months later, David Saro officially commissions Phase 1 of the Southstone Campus, inaugurating its flagship newsroom complex as the Mukhtar Bello Building to honour the founding editor. 
Delays associated with the naira float and fuel subsidy removal push the full build to 2025, but Phase 1 opens on schedule and anchors the Group's news and business operations on one site for the first time.
On air, CBC’s balanced election cycle coverage and real-time result verification draw international recognition, despite public criticism from ruling party stalwarts.
The same year brings a financial setback: the currency devaluation produces a ₦61bn foreign-exchange revaluation loss on dollar-denominated debt and content liabilities, causing the share price to fall by 30%.
Senator Ebikabowei Tamuno, defeated in his Bayelsa seat by Chief Preye Oyintare, accuses CBC News of election interference and partial coverage in Oyintare’s favour. The Editorial Committee opens CBC’s records to review, and the regulator finds no breach. 
2024 To absorb currency volatility and term-out short-term bank liabilities, Talatu issues a ₦150bn seven-year bond. 
The operational restructuring yields results: Odyssey+ reports its first profitable quarter since its launch, and Pinnacle renews its Super Eagles and European league package against competing foreign bids.
2025 In Lagos, the Southstone Campus is fully commissioned. The finished site brings the Group's newsroom, studios, post-production, and animation operations onto a single production hub on the Lekki–Epe Expressway. 
Talatu expands into digital entertainment with the launch of Talatu Interactive, an in-house games publisher that licenses the Group’s content library through Talatu Licensing  and commissions independent studios to develop games from it. Capitalizing on digital streaming subscriber gains and syndicated rights, Talatu closes FY2025 with record gross revenues of US$1.50bn. 
Talatu Africa Media takes a 40% equity interest in Cairo’s Red Sea Pictures, led by founder Layla Mansour, to produce Arabic-language series for Odyssey+.
The stake gives Talatu an Arabic-language production base and a route into Egyptian distribution, with an option to increase its holding (subject to regulatory approval).
Talatu files a competition complaint with the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission over access to premium sports content, arguing that the sub-licensing obligations imposed on Pinnacle's 2017 rights package must apply symmetrically across the market, and that the Canal+/MultiChoice combination, then awaiting completion, should be required to sub-license SuperSport's premium rights and wholesale feeds to rival broadcasters and streaming platforms including Pinnacle Sports and Odyssey+ on non-exclusive terms under the Nigerian Broadcasting Code.
2026 (to date): David Saro formally greenlights Odyssey+'s pan-African expansion, launching an Arabic-language tier in Egypt with Red Sea originals. The move follows Canal+’s completion of its acquisition of MultiChoice in late 2025, which brought DStv, Showmax and SuperSport into a group with reach across some 70 countries and a €100m African content fund. This places Odyssey+ and Pinnacle Sports in direct competition with a player able to subsidize content and sports rights across the continent
In Nigeria, Netflix's 2026 pullback of new commissions toward South Africa narrows the external market for Talatu Studios and Ironwood originals, deepening the Group's reliance on its own broadcast and streaming ecosystem.
The FCCPC’s inquiry into premium sports rights remains open, with the Commission having taken evidence from both Talatu and the Canal+/MultiChoice entity. Separately, the NBC fines CBC ₦3bn after finding that Pinnacle offered the sub-licence required under its 2017 conditions only after the opening weeks of the 2024–25 European league season, and at a price rivals described as prohibitive. Talatu accepts the finding on timing, appeals the penalty, and settles at ₦1.2bn. Canal+/MultiChoice cites the fine in its FCCPC defense, arguing that Talatu is asking for obligations it has itself failed to meet. Talatu’s reply is that the breach was one of delay and that the episode shows why such obligations must be enforceable and symmetric across the market.

Note on the Chairmanship: The Board is chaired by Anwar Alhassan as Executive Chairman, which departs from the NCCG 2018 preference for a non-executive chair. The Company explains the departure by his position as its largest individual shareholder and by his defined remit: capital allocation, shareholder relations, Group strategy and stewardship of the Group’s intellectual property and rights portfolio. He has no operating or editorial role, which remain with the CEO and the Editorial Committee. Dr. Bukola Adewale, as Lead Independent Director, provides the independent counterweight.

Executive Profile: Anwar Alhassan
Born in Lagos on 7 April 1970, Anwar Faisal Alhassan is the eldest of three siblings (alongside Amira and Aisha) born to Faisal and Fauziya Alhassan. He completed his secondary education at King’s College, Lagos, before earning a BSc in Economics from Ahmadu Bello University Zaria, in 1992.
Outside the Talatu boardroom, Anwar wholly owns and chairs Westbridge Asset Management and  Westbridge Investments Limited run day-to-day by Mohammed Kolo, He co-owns Northstar Investments Limited with his sisters. Led by Fatima Nimbo and estimated at over US$800m in net asset value, it holds positions across banking, technology, hospitality and real estate, including stakes in GTCO Plc, Naspers and Flutterwave, alongside fund commitments with Helios Investment Partners and direct property holdings.
